Convergence of Markov Chains

published May 20, 2024, updated October 25, 2026

Many Markov chains forget where they started: run them for long enough, and they seem to not depend on the initial state. This is what makes long-run averages of a chain meaningful, and also makes sampling methods like Markov Chain Monte Carlo viable.

This is a neat little proof of the requisite proposition: an irreducible aperiodic Markov Chain with an invariant distribution π\piπ converges in distribution to π\piπ.

Suppose a transition matrix PPP on a countable state space III is irreducible and aperiodic, and moreover that it admits an invariant distribution π\piπ. Then for a Markov Chain (Xn)∼Markov(λ,P)(X_n) \sim \mathrm{Markov}(\lambda, P)(Xn​)∼Markov(λ,P) for any starting distribution λ\lambdaλ, we have

P[Xn=i]→πi as n→∞ \mathbb{P}\left[ X_n = i \right] \to \pi_i \text{ as } n \to \infty P[Xn​=i]→πi​ as n→∞

This proof uses a technique called coupling.
